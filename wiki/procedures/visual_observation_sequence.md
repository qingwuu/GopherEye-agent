# Visual Observation Sequence

This page defines the first-pass visual inspection sequence for grape leaf
images. The purpose is to collect botanical observations before diagnosis.

## Principle

Do not start with a disease name. Start with visible plant evidence.

```text
observe
localize
separate normal from abnormal
compare with candidate diseases
decide what is still missing
```

## Step 1: Confirm Leaf And Grape Plausibility

Check whether the image shows:

```text
a leaf blade rather than only soil, fruit, stem, or background
grape-like lobing or blade form when visible
serrated margin when visible
primary veins radiating from the petiole region when visible
petiole attachment or leaf base when visible
```

If the image does not show a leaf, ask for a grape leaf image. If the image
shows a leaf but grape identity is uncertain, say that grape evidence is
insufficient.

## Step 2: Check Image Quality

Assess whether the symptomatic area can be inspected.

Poor-quality blockers include:

```text
blur at the lesion or fungal growth area
overexposure that removes yellow, brown, white, or gray detail
shadow that hides surface texture
leaf folded over the symptomatic region
symptom too small or too distant
background objects mistaken for leaf symptoms
```

If quality blocks inspection, ask for a clearer image of the same surface and
same symptomatic area.

## Step 3: Identify Leaf Surface

Use:

```text
adaxial: upper surface
abaxial: lower surface
mixed: both surfaces visible in one image
uncertain: surface cannot be judged
not_leaf: image does not contain a leaf
```

Surface matters because fungal growth, vein relief, chlorosis, and sporulation
may be more visible on one side than the other.

## Step 4: Identify Visible Structures

Use consistent anatomical terms:

```text
blade / lamina
margin
apex
base
petiole
midrib
primary veins
secondary veins
interveinal areas
```

If key structures are absent from the frame, note the limit.

## Step 5: Separate Normal From Abnormal

Do not treat these alone as disease evidence:

```text
normal vein contrast
raised veins on the lower surface
serrated margin
minor curling from handling
light reflection on the upper surface
shadows along veins
small mechanical tears without disease pattern
```

Abnormal signs may include:

```text
chlorosis
necrosis
powdery surface growth
downy or fuzzy underside growth
water-soaked areas
vein-bounded spots
yellow halos
dark fruiting bodies or dots
distortion, puckering, or galls
```

## Step 6: Produce An Observation Summary

A useful observation summary should say:

```text
what surface is visible
whether quality is sufficient
where symptoms are located
what abnormal signs are visible
what normal features should not be over-read
what evidence is missing
```

# Symptom Localization Procedure

This procedure defines how to describe where symptoms appear on a grape leaf.
Precise localization is required before disease comparison.

## Location Vocabulary

Use anatomical and spatial terms together:

```text
adaxial surface
abaxial surface
upper lamina
lower lamina
near midrib
near primary veins
interveinal area
leaf margin
leaf apex
leaf base
petiolar sinus region
scattered across blade
clustered in one region
```

## Pattern Vocabulary

Describe the pattern, not only the color:

```text
round spots
angular or vein-bounded lesions
irregular blotches
marginal scorch
diffuse chlorosis
yellow halo
necrotic center
oil spot
angular vein-limited lesion
powdery coating
fine web-like growth
white-gray powdery colony
downy or fuzzy growth
white cottony sporulation
dark chasmothecia
sparse sporulation
vein tracking
raised or distorted tissue
```

## Surface Relationship

For each visible symptom, state whether it is:

```text
visible only on the adaxial side
visible only on the abaxial side
aligned across both sides of the same leaf
stronger on one side
impossible to compare because one side is missing
```

Surface relationship is especially important when differentiating powdery
surface growth, underside sporulation, chlorotic upper-surface lesions, and
normal lower-surface vein relief.

One missing side should be recorded as a limitation, not as automatic
insufficiency. After localizing symptoms, decide whether the visible surface is
already diagnostic for the disease-specific rule.

## Vein Relationship

Record whether symptoms:

```text
follow veins
are bounded by veins
cross veins freely
cluster near primary veins
appear mainly in interveinal tissue
are unrelated to vein pattern
```

Vein relationship helps separate true lesions from shadows, normal vein relief,
and some abiotic patterns.

## Distribution Across The Leaf

Record distribution:

```text
single lesion
few scattered lesions
many scattered lesions
localized patch
widespread across lamina
mostly marginal
mostly basal
mostly apical
```

Distribution should be considered together with image framing. A close-up may
not show whether symptoms are local or widespread.

## Missing Localization Evidence

Ask for more evidence when:

```text
the symptomatic area is out of focus
only a close-up is available and distribution is unknown
only one side is visible when surface comparison is important
the lesion edge is not visible
veins are out of frame
the image cannot separate leaf symptoms from background artifacts
```
